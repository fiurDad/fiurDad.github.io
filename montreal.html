Dad's Research

I've been doing a lot of research on this and I think we have a genuinely great trip here either way. Both options start in Montreal. I've been interested in Montreal for a while — it's got a real arts and literary scene, great museums, good bookshops, and the neighborhoods are walkable and interesting. July 1 is Canada Day, which I didn't plan but worked out perfectly.

Option A uses the Amtrak train from New York to Montreal, which I think would be a cool experience. Option B skips that and goes straight to Montreal, then takes a Canadian train to Toronto. I'll lay out both. I lean toward Option B but I want you to see the whole picture.

🍁

July 1 is Canada Day

I didn't plan this but it worked out great. Both options have us in Canada on their biggest national holiday. Fireworks, music, the whole thing. Option A puts us in Montreal for the full day. Option B has us in Toronto. Either way, good timing.

The Two Plans
Here's What I Found
Option A

The Train Idea

Fly to New York, take the scenic Amtrak to Montreal, fly home from there.

IND → New York City → Amtrak → Montreal → IND
1
Sunday, June 28

New York — One Night

Delta nonstop from Indy gets us to LaGuardia mid-morning. We have the afternoon. I was thinking the Strand bookstore, or just walk around Midtown. Early night because the train leaves at 7:15am.

2
Monday, June 29

The Adirondack Train

This is the part I think would be really cool. The train goes through the Hudson Valley, the Adirondack Mountains, along Lake Champlain. Bring a book, obviously. It's about 11 hours and we get into Montreal Central in the evening. I've wanted to do this ride for a while.

3
Tuesday, June 30

Montreal: Museums and Theater

I want to spend the morning at the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts — it's a real collection, not a tourist trap. Afternoon in Mile End, which is the literary neighborhood. There's an indie bookshop there called Drawn & Quarterly that I've read a lot about. And Les Miserables is playing at Theatre St-Denis that week, which seems like a good fit.

4
Wednesday, July 1 — Canada Day

Canada Day in Montreal

The whole city is out. I want to walk the Plateau and Saint-Laurent. I also want to go to La Grande Bibliotheque — it's Montreal's main public library but also just a genuinely interesting building. Fireworks over the St. Lawrence at night. I think this would be a good day.

5
Thursday, July 2

Fly Home

Delta from Montreal through Atlanta or Detroit. We'd be home by early afternoon.

Places I Want to Go — Option A
  • Montreal Museum of Fine Arts — serious collection, not touristy
  • Drawn & Quarterly bookshop, Mile End — one of the best indie bookshops in North America apparently
  • La Grande Bibliotheque — Montreal's main library, also just a cool building
  • Pointe-a-Calliere Museum — archaeology museum built over actual excavated ruins in Old Montreal
  • Les Miserables at Theatre St-Denis — running through mid-July
  • Saint-Laurent and the Plateau on foot
i

One thing to know about the train: The Adirondack has had some summer service issues the last couple of years. It's running right now and I'm watching it. I'd want to confirm it's still on track around June 15 before we commit. If it's not running, we just do Option B instead — no big deal.

Option B — My Preference

Montreal and Toronto

Fly straight to Montreal, take a Canadian train to Toronto, fly home from there.

IND → Montreal → VIA Rail → Toronto → IND
1
Sunday, June 28

Arrive in Montreal

Delta through Detroit gets us in before noon. I want to just walk the Mile End and Plateau neighborhoods in the afternoon. This is where Leonard Cohen grew up, where a lot of artists and writers still live. It's not a tourist neighborhood, which is exactly why I want to go there first.

2
Monday, June 29

Full Day in Montreal

Morning at the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts. Then Old Montreal for the afternoon — the cobblestone streets, galleries, the waterfront. I want to find the Drawn & Quarterly bookshop in Mile End before we leave. Evening: Les Miserables is playing at Theatre St-Denis, which I think would be worth going to.

3
Tuesday, June 30

Train to Toronto

We take the VIA Rail train to Toronto mid-morning. I looked into Business Class and it's worth it — they serve a real meal, it's about 5 hours, and it's a comfortable ride. Toronto Union Station is one of those grand old train halls that's just nice to arrive into. I was thinking dinner in the Distillery District that night — it's a historic industrial area that now has galleries and restaurants.

4
Wednesday, July 1 — Canada Day

Canada Day in Toronto

I want to go to the Art Gallery of Ontario in the morning. It's in a Frank Gehry building and the collection is really strong — Group of Seven, contemporary Canadian art. In the afternoon I want to find Type Books or BMV on Bloor. Toronto has good independent bookshops. That evening there should be a show at one of the Mirvish theaters on King Street — Toronto's theater scene is legitimately good.

5
Thursday, July 2

Fly Home

Air Canada has a nonstop from Toronto to Indianapolis. About 2 hours. We'd be home by mid-afternoon.

Places I Want to Go — Option B
  • Drawn & Quarterly, Montreal — consistently listed as one of the best indie bookshops in North America
  • Montreal Museum of Fine Arts — I've been reading about their collection
  • Les Miserables at Theatre St-Denis — running through mid-July
  • Art Gallery of Ontario — Frank Gehry redesign, strong Canadian collection
  • Type Books / BMV, Toronto — good independent bookshops on Bloor
  • Mirvish theaters, King Street — Toronto takes theater seriously
  • Distillery District — historic area, now galleries and restaurants
✓

Why I prefer this one: Two cities, more ground covered, and the Canadian train between them is reliable. No 7am stress the morning after we fly in. And if the Amtrak situation with Option A doesn't work out, this is just the plan anyway.

One More Thing
Schwartz's. On the List.

This trip is going to have plenty of room for both of us to put things on the agenda. That's the whole point. Schwartz's is mine — I've had it on my list for about 20 years. It's been on Boulevard Saint-Laurent since 1928 and people still line up outside. It's primarily a smoked meat place so the menu won't be for everyone, but I'd love it if we could at least stop by. Fries and pickles are pretty great too.

Schwartz's Hebrew Delicatessen  ·  3895 Boulevard Saint-Laurent, Montreal
